How to play

  • Objective: Collect more seeds than Kreedu.
  • Sow: Tap a pit on your (bottom) row. Its seeds are lifted and dropped one at a time into each following pit — rightwards along your row, then leftwards along Kreedu's (counterclockwise).
  • Keep going: If the last seed lands in a pit that already had seeds, pick that pit up and keep sowing.
  • Four is a harvest: Any pit that reaches exactly four seeds during your sow is taken into your store.
  • Opposite capture: If your last seed lands in an empty pit on your side, you take the opposite pit too.
  • The round ends when one side has no seeds left to sow. Leftover seeds on the board go to that side's owner.

The circle of sowing and capture

The board is two rows of seven pits facing each other — your row and Kreedu's — and seeds move in one direction only: counterclockwise around the whole ring. There is no die and no chance move. Every seed you sow is a small, deliberate act of counting.

Rules played here

Each side begins with five seeds in every one of its seven pits — seventy seeds on the board.
A pit that reaches exactly four seeds during a sow is harvested on the spot, even mid-turn.
The round ends the moment a side has no seeds left to sow. Whatever remains on the board is swept into stores by ownership.
Whoever holds more seeds when play stops wins the round.
Vaamana Guntalu is played under many regional names and rule sets across South India — starting seed counts, sowing direction, and capture conditions all vary by household and region. This board plays the common Telugu four-seed harvest version, sown counterclockwise from five starting seeds per pit.
