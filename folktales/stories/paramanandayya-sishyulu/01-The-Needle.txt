The Seven Naive Disciples

Guru Paramanandayya once needed a needle to mend his torn clothes. He sent his seven naive disciples to his friend Ramiah in the nearby village of Rapur to fetch one.

On their way, they came across a flooded stream. One disciple said, “The stream is angry and will eat us if we cross now. Let us wait until it falls asleep at night.” The others agreed and waited until evening. When the water became calm, they crossed silently, believing the stream was asleep.

They reached Rapur at midnight and asked Ramiah for a needle. When he gave it to one disciple, the others objected. “Give it to all of us equally. Otherwise, we may quarrel!”

Ramiah explained that he had only one needle, but the disciples refused to accept it. His wife then pointed to a large wooden log and said, “Pin the needle to the log and carry it together.”

The disciples happily followed her advice. Six carried the heavy log while the seventh carefully pinned the needle to it. They hurried back, worried that the stream might wake up.

When they reached home, they proudly presented the log to Guru Paramanandayya. The Guru scolded them for their naivety.

Soon, heavy rain made the firewood in the backyard useless. The disciples chopped the log into pieces, which were then used as firewood to cook their meal.

Guru Paramanandayya smiled. Their naivety had finally served a useful purpose.

Teaching: Even a naive act can sometimes lead to a useful result.
